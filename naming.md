# Naming — Klarity Continuity Replacement

## 1. Top pick

### **Throughline**

A throughline is the sentence that holds a story together across chapters. That's exactly what this product does — it carries the patient's own breakthrough sentence from one session to the next, and into the days in between. It's warm, literary, and structural without being clinical; one word, easy to say once and remember; works as both noun and product verb ("send it to Throughline," "Throughline picked it up").

> *"Built on Klarity, Throughline is the AI that holds a patient's own words between sessions — so the breakthrough doesn't get lost on the way to the next one."*

---

## 2. Runners-up

### 2. **Tether**
A soft line back to the moment that mattered. Evokes anchoring without being literal about it (we already use "anchor" inside the product). Trade-off vs. Throughline: slightly more abstract — doesn't telegraph *story* or *continuity* the way Throughline does. Better as a feature name than a product name.

### 3. **Recall**
Plain English for what the system does: it recalls the patient's own sentence at the right moment. Verb-able, two syllables, free of jargon. Trade-off vs. Throughline: Loom-aligned word energy, but it's a common dictionary word — harder to own as a brand mark, and search SEO is brutal.

### 4. **Holdfast**
A holdfast is the part of a plant that grips a rock through the tide. Beautiful image for a between-sessions memory layer that keeps a patient grounded. Trade-off vs. Throughline: two-syllable compound feels slightly older/British; risks being "cute" where Throughline is structural.

### 5. **Carry**
The product's whole job in one verb. A patient *carries* their sentence through the week; the system *carries* the story between providers. Trade-off vs. Throughline: too soft and too common to stand alone in a logo — would need a wordmark treatment to feel like a product, not a feeling.

## 4. Anti-list

- **Klarity Continuity** — the working title. Sponsor name plus a noun. Tells you the category but not the wedge; reads like a feature, not a product.
- **Echo** — captures the "your words back to you" idea, but already owned hard by Amazon, and "echo" in mental health connotes rumination, which is the opposite of what the anchor does.
- **Anchor** — strong word, but it's already the name of a feature inside the product (the daily card). Promoting it to product-level would collapse the vocabulary and make the demo confusing.
